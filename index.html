The idea we're testing: FirstBatch

Before you place your first order of 50–500 units, an engineer checks your parts list and your supplier, and tells you what could go wrong. We'd look at:

  • Parts. Which parts are short, have long lead times, or are being discontinued. A part that is in stock for 5 units can take months at 500.
  • Supplier. Whether they build in-house or quietly subcontract abroad, where your units actually ship from, and any customs or duty delays.

If this turns out to be useful, a later step could be placing the batch with verified UK small-batch makers. Things like EMC testing would only come in if people tell us they matter.

This is only an idea. Nothing exists yet, and we don't know if this problem is real for you. That's what we're asking.

Are you a UK maker, assembler or test lab?

We're also testing a second idea. More buyers now ask an AI assistant to find and shortlist suppliers ("find a UK firm that can assemble 200 boards"), and AI buying agents are starting to send enquiries and request quotes on their own. Small UK firms often don't show up in those answers, even when they're a good fit.

  • Findable. We check whether AI assistants name your firm for the jobs you actually want, and fix what stops them.
  • Quotable. Your capabilities, minimum orders, lead times and certifications set out so an AI agent can read them and send a usable enquiry.
  • Trusted. Basic security and compliance answers ready, so you're not ruled out by a questionnaire.

Again, only an idea. We'd like to know if you've had enquiries that started with an AI tool, and how new customers find you today.

Who we'd like to hear from

  • Hardware founders with a prototype or first product
  • Engineers who have run a small production batch
  • UK electronics makers and assemblers
  • EMC test labs, incubators and hackspaces

What we'll ask

The survey asks you to rate statements like "Parts shortages or long lead times delayed my first batch", and asks what you would want checked about a supplier before ordering. Makers and labs get a short section on how customers find them and whether AI tools play a part. The chat asks about the last time you needed more units than you could build yourself: what was hardest, what it cost you, and what you tried.
